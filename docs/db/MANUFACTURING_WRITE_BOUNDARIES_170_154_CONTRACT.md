# Proposal — manufacturing write boundaries after M192 (#170, #154, #229)

**Status:** revised review contract, not implemented and not permission to change Production or expose employee consumption. Anchor: `main@76689dc076ca50f1765603bf7fb17439e4d0af39`. The 2026-09-28 independent read-only catalog review found M190/M191/M192 each once. The grant and FK observations below are catalog findings, not proof of a live destructive incident; reproduce the cascade on disposable PostgreSQL 17 before claiming its precise outcome. Staging remains unverified.

## Immediate integrity dependency: deletion and cascades

`material_consumption.work_order_id` has `ON DELETE CASCADE` to `work_orders`. The reviewed `work_orders_delete_policy` checks for an organization membership row without checking `is_active`, and direct DELETE grants exist for `authenticated` and `anon`. M192's revoke on direct `material_consumption` writes does not guard a deletion executed by the FK action. A delete of a work order with `POSTED` consumption could erase the consumption rows while its receipt, SLE/bin and WIP effects remain. Deleting its MO may reach the same cascade; test actual FK order and outcome instead of assuming the MO FK prevents it. This is a **live catalog exposure to resolve in the first protective migration**, separately from the later UI rollout. Do not treat this document as the fix.

The first implementation PR must deny DELETE of a WO or MO with posted or otherwise financially effective consumption before any FK cascade can remove it. Prefer a durable database guard, together with removal of direct client DELETE grants and relevant policies, covering every authorized RPC and direct route; decide separately how to retain historical released/expired/cancelled reservations. Inspect every incoming FK and CASCADE path, including TRUNCATE permissions. A disposable PG17 test must build a valid posted M192 event, attempt direct WO DELETE and MO DELETE as a member (and any callable indirect delete), assert the specific denial and no changes in `material_consumption`, the receipt and its `consumption_ids`, stock ledger, bins, WIP and GL. Verify the guard with a DB owner/privileged RPC path as well as RLS, and check that the intended history-preserving administrative workflow is defined. Until this closes, avoid deleting affected WO/MO rows in Production.

## Authority and observed write surfaces

The active same-org Org Admin may grant exact permissions through the RBAC UI; an employee receives only explicitly assigned operations. Check membership activity, role expiry, organization ownership, lifecycle, amounts and audit at the database transaction boundary. Org Admin bypass may pass authorization but never structural or lifecycle checks. `service_role` stays server-only. Existing `manufacturing.orders.create` / `.update` do not authorize the new operations by implication.

The independent 2026-09-28 catalog review reports: `authenticated` has INSERT, UPDATE, DELETE and TRUNCATE table grants on `material_reservations`, `work_orders` and `stage_wip_log`; `anon` has full DML on reservations, work orders and `manufacturing_orders` but no table grants on stage WIP; no PUBLIC table grants were found. Recheck table, column, inherited and default grants at implementation time. RLS on the first three public manufacturing tables is `TO public`. WO policies accept an inactive membership row; reservation and MO policies use `auth_org_id()` (a `LIMIT 1` selector with no order), whereas stage WIP uses `wardah_org_id()`. Absent an explicit `WITH CHECK`, PostgreSQL reuses `USING` for applicable updates. A grant or zero-row mutation alone establishes neither permission nor denial. A user in two organizations without a trusted org selection must fail closed; derive scope from the target MO and validate every child org.

## Binding lock and permission rules

For every writer touching an MO and its children, derive the organization from the target MO, validate it, lock the MO `FOR UPDATE` **before** any WO, stage WIP, reservation, product or bin lock, and revalidate scope after locking. For M192 events only, take the `(MO-derived org_id,event_id)` event advisory lock **before** MO; then policy `FOR SHARE`, WO, stage WIP, reservations, products, bins in M192's order. Reserve, release, WO start/complete and status transitions must conform to the same MO-first child prefix. In particular, reservation INSERT FK checks on the MO and WO UPDATE triggers must not establish product→MO or WO→MO waits. Rewrite or neutralize the `work_orders.trigger_update_mo_status` path together with #230; merely adding permission checks to `start_operation` and `complete_operation` is insufficient. Exercise real two-session commit and rollback races: reserve vs consume, release vs consume, WO start/complete vs consume, two starts on one WO, and policy change vs consume. Assert actual wait relation and no `40P01`, double effects, stale overwrite or partial pause.

A new permission row is created with **zero explicit `role_permissions` rows**, but this alone does not prove least privilege: `create_role_from_template` matches keys by `LIKE`; the `production_manager` template's `manufacturing.%` would include later manufacturing keys, and Org Admin bypass applies to nonsensitive keys. Existing `Full Access` already includes the M190 consume key in the reviewed organization. Decide and test template behavior for new roles, whether terminal and WIP-cost keys are sensitive, and the Org Admin bypass before selecting key names. Test a new role created from every affected template; test existing roles separately. Do not silently grant via a template or infer authority from `manufacturing.orders.create/update`.

## Migration and rollout gates

1. Take a fresh inventory of direct writers, callable RPCs (including owner/security mode and EXECUTE ACL), triggers, FK cascades, grants on all columns and effective RLS `USING`/`WITH CHECK`. Prioritize the posted-consumption deletion guard as its own first protective migration with an independent review and PG17 proof, rather than waiting for all employee UI or #260.
2. Write failing PG17 acceptance on **valid populated rows**. Cover direct INSERT/UPDATE/DELETE/TRUNCATE and FK cascades; assert SQLSTATE, reason and unchanged effects. Cover active Org Admin with invalid input, granted/ungranted employees, inactive membership, inactive/expired role, revoked grant, foreign org and mismatched child→MO org, two-org user without org claim, and historical released reservations. Check `has_table_privilege` / `has_column_privilege` for effective INSERT, UPDATE, DELETE, TRUNCATE, REFERENCES and TRIGGER; use behavioral tests too.
3. Introduce exact keys and guarded RPCs with pinned `search_path`, explicit EXECUTE revokes from PUBLIC/anon and from authenticated for internal helpers, actor/org checks, mandatory MO-first locks and audit entries. New callable RPCs get only the intended `authenticated` EXECUTE. The direct-write closure must revoke `INSERT, UPDATE, DELETE, TRUNCATE, REFERENCES, TRIGGER` from PUBLIC, anon and authenticated where applicable and remove permissive policies/column grants; keep reviewed read needs. Do not alter M190–M192 bytes.
4. Enumerate and migrate each caller before revoking its old grant: `inventory-transaction-service.ts` reserve/release and retired consume, `mesService.ts` pause and stale direct consume, `updateWorkOrderStatus`, `stageWipLogService` create/update and `supabase-service.ts` close/delete, MO create/transition paths. Include tests for Admin granting/revoking, employee behavior and event-id retry. The DB-first compatibility interval, if needed, lasts **at most one reviewed client release** between additive RPC deployment and a dedicated grant-revocation migration; no new user-facing capability during that interval. Both deployments require dated checkpoints. Exit only after client usage telemetry/readback finds no callers of retired paths, browser tests and PG17 gates pass, and the revocation migration is deployed and verified. If the exit gate fails, stop rollout and retain the old UI disabled; do not treat an open grant as steady state.
5. Require exact-head Fresh DB on PG17 with M190–M192 acceptance, concurrency tests, actual DELETE cascade negative control, SQLSTATE checks and independent review. Each Production migration needs a current backup, readback and owner approval; employee consumption stays unmounted until real same-org employee-session permission and replay tests pass.
